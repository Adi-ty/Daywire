The referenced lesson is the only factual premise. Test one clear learning objective per question, preferably a cause, mechanism, or explicit distinction. Every question must have exactly one correct answer without relying on outside knowledge.
Design the correct answer and the distractors together. Check each option independently against the precise question: one must be supported; every distractor must be clearly wrong for that question, not merely less comprehensive. Avoid overlapping alternatives, nested combinations (A, B, both A and B), all/none of the above, trick wording, and absolutes such as always or guarantees unless the lesson supports them. If the lesson offers several valid mitigations, do not ask which single mitigation works; ask about a mechanism or distinguishing condition instead. Keep the explanation grounded in the lesson and make clear why the answer fits. Do not invent a technical claim just to make a distractor sound plausible.
